**A Software Architect** is a senior technical role responsible for designing the high-level structure of software systems so they meet functional and non-functional requirements (scalability, performance, security, maintainability, reliability, etc.).

They focus on the “big picture” rather than day-to-day coding of individual features. The role sits between business/product needs and the engineering teams that implement the system.

### Core Responsibilities
- Define the overall system architecture, including major components, their interactions, data flows, and integration points.
- Select technology stacks, frameworks, platforms, and patterns (e.g., microservices vs. monolith, event-driven architecture, CQRS, etc.).
- Address non-functional requirements: performance, scalability, availability, security, observability, and cost.
- Create architectural diagrams, decision records (ADRs), and technical roadmaps.
- Guide and mentor development teams, review designs/code for architectural compliance, and resolve cross-cutting technical issues.
- Evaluate trade-offs and make technical decisions that balance short-term delivery with long-term sustainability.
- Collaborate with stakeholders (product managers, business leaders, other architects) to align technical solutions with business goals.
- Identify technical risks and plan mitigation strategies.
- Sometimes contribute to coding critical or foundational parts of the system, though this varies by organization.

### Common Types of Software Architects
- **Application / Software Architect** — Focuses on a single application or product.
- **Solution Architect** — Designs end-to-end solutions that may span multiple systems, often more client- or project-facing.
- **Enterprise Architect** — Works at a broader organizational level, defining standards, platforms, and roadmaps across many systems.
- **Domain / Technical Architect** — Specializes in a particular area (e.g., data, security, cloud, AI/ML).

### Key Skills
- Deep technical knowledge across multiple domains (software design patterns, distributed systems, cloud platforms, databases, APIs, security, etc.).
- Strong system-thinking and abstraction skills.
- Ability to communicate complex technical concepts clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.
- Decision-making under uncertainty and trade-off analysis.
- Leadership and influence without always having direct managerial authority.
- Understanding of business context and product constraints.

### How It Differs from Related Roles
| Role                  | Primary Focus                          | Typical Scope                  |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Software Engineer / Developer | Implementing features and writing code | Individual components or features |
| Tech Lead             | Day-to-day technical leadership of a team | One team or project            |
| Software Architect    | High-level system design and technical strategy | One or more systems            |
| Engineering Manager   | People management, delivery, process   | Team or department             |

The exact title and responsibilities vary significantly by company size and culture. In smaller organizations the architect may still write a lot of code; in large enterprises the role can be more pure design, governance, and strategy.

In short, a Software Architect is the person who ensures the software system is built on a solid, coherent technical foundation that can evolve successfully over time.
