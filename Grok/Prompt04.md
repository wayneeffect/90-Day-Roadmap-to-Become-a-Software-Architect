**A realistic 90-day roadmap accelerates your path toward a Software Architect role but will not fully qualify most people for the title from a junior level.** Software Architect positions typically require 5–15+ years of strong engineering experience with demonstrated architectural impact. Treat this as an intensive skill-building, portfolio, and positioning plan (assuming you already have mid-level development experience). Adjust intensity based on your available time (target 10–15+ focused hours per week).

### Overall Goals for the 90 Days
- Build deep knowledge of architecture principles, patterns, and trade-offs.
- Practice system design and create tangible artifacts (diagrams, ADRs, designs).
- Strengthen cloud, distributed systems, and non-functional requirements skills.
- Improve communication, documentation, and influence skills.
- Update your professional presence and prepare to operate as (or interview for) an architect.

### Phase 1: Days 1–30 — Foundations & Architect Mindset
**Focus**: Shift from “how to implement” to “how to design and decide.”

**Key activities**:
- Study core concepts: What software architecture is, quality attributes (scalability, availability, performance, security, maintainability, observability), and trade-offs (CAP theorem, consistency models, coupling/cohesion).
- Master design principles: SOLID, DRY, KISS, YAGNI, Clean Architecture / Hexagonal (Ports & Adapters).
- Learn essential patterns: Layered, microservices, event-driven, CQRS, event sourcing, modular monolith vs microservices.
- Practice reading and critiquing real architectures (open-source projects, company tech blogs, case studies).
- Start writing Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) for small decisions in your current work or personal projects.
- Begin a learning journal: Document one architectural insight or trade-off daily.

**Recommended resources**:
- Books: *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* (Martin Kleppmann) — start this; *Clean Architecture* (Robert C. Martin); *Fundamentals of Software Architecture* (Richards & Ford).
- Free: roadmap.sh/software-architect, ByteByteGo materials, C4 model documentation.
- Practice: Sketch high-level designs for familiar systems (e.g., a simple e-commerce or social feed).

**Weekly checkpoints**: By day 30, be able to explain 5–7 major architectural patterns with real trade-offs and produce basic C4 diagrams + ADRs.

### Phase 2: Days 31–60 — Deep Practice & Hands-On Design
**Focus**: Apply knowledge to realistic problems and build cloud/distributed systems fluency.

**Key activities**:
- Intensive system design practice: Design systems end-to-end (URL shortener, chat app, e-commerce checkout, notification service, etc.). Cover requirements gathering, high-level design, data modeling, APIs, scaling, failure modes, and non-functional requirements.
- Cloud architecture: Pick one major cloud (AWS recommended for breadth) and learn core services + common patterns (high availability, serverless, event-driven, multi-AZ, etc.).
- Hands-on project: Design and partially implement (or fully document) a moderately complex system. Produce full artifacts: context diagrams, container diagrams, component diagrams, sequence diagrams, ADRs, risk analysis, and technology recommendations.
- Study observability, security, and data architecture basics.
- Soft skills: Practice explaining designs to non-technical stakeholders; run mock architecture reviews.

**Recommended resources**:
- System design: Grokking the System Design Interview (or free alternatives), ByteByteGo, Alex Xu books.
- Cloud: AWS Solutions Architect Associate materials or free tier labs; equivalent for Azure/GCP if preferred.
- Tools: Draw.io / Excalidraw / Miro for diagrams; PlantUML or Structurizr for code-based diagrams.

**Weekly checkpoints**: Complete 4–6 full system designs. Finish a substantial portfolio piece. Start (or complete) a cloud certification study path if it fits your target roles.

### Phase 3: Days 61–90 — Application, Visibility & Transition
**Focus**: Create evidence of architectural capability and position yourself for the role.

**Key activities**:
- Portfolio & professional branding: Publish 2–3 architecture case studies (anonymized if from work, or personal/open-source). Update LinkedIn, resume, and GitHub with architecture-focused language and artifacts. Write short posts or blog entries explaining a design decision or trade-off.
- Real-world practice: Volunteer for design discussions, tech spikes, or architectural reviews at your current job. Mentor others on design. Propose improvements to existing systems with documented rationale.
- Networking & learning in public: Engage in architecture communities (e.g., relevant Slack/Discord, conferences, or online forums). Follow and interact with architects on professional platforms.
- Interview & role preparation: Practice architecture interviews (whiteboard system design + behavioral questions about past decisions). Research target companies’ tech stacks and architecture styles. Begin applying to senior engineer / tech lead / architect roles that match your current level, or seek internal opportunities.
- Optional certification push: Finish AWS Solutions Architect Associate (or equivalent) if started earlier — useful signal, not a requirement.
- Reflection & next 90 days: Assess gaps and plan continued learning (deeper domain expertise, enterprise architecture, leadership, etc.).

**Weekly checkpoints**: Have a polished portfolio, updated professional profiles, at least one internal or external architecture contribution, and a shortlist of target roles/companies.

### Daily/Weekly Habits to Maintain
- 30–60 minutes deliberate learning + 1–2 hours practice/design work most days.
- One design critique or ADR every week.
- One communication practice (explaining a design out loud or in writing) weekly.
- Review progress every Sunday and adjust.

### Realistic Expectations & Tips
- Success depends heavily on your starting point. With solid mid-level experience and consistent effort, you can significantly raise your architectural maturity and visibility in 90 days.
- Prioritize depth over breadth — better to deeply understand a few patterns and trade-offs than skim many.
- Soft skills (communication, influence without authority, business alignment) often differentiate architects more than pure technical knowledge.
- Continue after day 90: The real transition usually involves years of progressive responsibility.

This plan draws from established architecture roadmaps and practical career advice. Track your work publicly where possible — it compounds. If you share your current experience level or target specialization (cloud, domain-driven, enterprise, etc.), the plan can be refined further.
